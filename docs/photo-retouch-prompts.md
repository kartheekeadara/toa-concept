# Taste of Europe: photo retouch prompts for ChatGPT

15 real store photos, numbered in the order they matter (hero first). Every photo is the full-resolution original.

## How to use this

1. Start a **new ChatGPT chat for each photo**. Long chats drift and start changing things you didn't ask for.
2. Paste the **master prompt** below, attach the photo, then paste that photo's own prompt underneath.
3. **For a photo that only needs things removed, use ChatGPT's select/brush edit tool if you have it.** Paint over just the sign, tag or person and type "remove this, rebuild what's behind it". It leaves the rest of the photo untouched, which is the safest way to keep labels readable.
4. Check the result against the checklist at the bottom. If any label text is wrong, reply: "The text on [product] changed. Redo it and copy the original label exactly." If it's still wrong after two tries, use the brush tool or skip that photo.
5. Download at the largest size offered and save it with **the same filename** (e.g. `05-deli-case-3.jpg`). Send the files back to me, and I'll put them in `public/photos/real-edited/` and switch the site over.

**Size note:** ChatGPT usually returns about 1,500px on the long side. That's fine for most photos, but the 4 hero photos (01–04) fill the whole screen. Run those 4 through a free upscaler like Upscayl (2×) afterwards.

## Master prompt (paste first in every chat)

```
You are retouching a real photograph of a small European grocery store, Taste of Europe Gourmet Foods in Charlotte, NC, for its website. The goal is the same photo, as if a professional photographer had shot it on a good day. It must still be clearly the same store, with the same products in the same places.

Hard rules:
1. Do NOT add, remove, move, resize or swap any product, package, basket, shelf, fixture or piece of furniture, unless I list it below.
2. Copy every printed word on every package and sign EXACTLY as it is in the original, letter for letter, including Polish, Cyrillic and Georgian text. Do not translate, re-letter, invent or "tidy up" any text. If you can't render a label exactly, leave that area as close to the original pixels as possible.
3. Do not add people, hands, props, food, steam, garnish, decorations, light rays, bokeh balls, lens flare or text.
4. Keep the same camera angle, framing and aspect ratio. Only crop slightly if straightening needs it. I will do final crops myself.
5. Keep it photographic and natural. Real wood grain, real wicker texture, real marble floor, real packaging gloss. No plastic, waxy, over-smoothed, HDR or CGI look. No heavy vignette, no filter look.
6. When you remove something, rebuild what was behind it so it continues naturally (shelf edge, basket weave, wall, floor), with no smudges or blurry patches.

General look for every photo: neutral-warm white balance (remove the green and blue fluorescent cast, so whites look clean white, not yellow), gently lifted shadows, natural contrast, crisp but not over-sharpened detail, colors true to the packaging, nothing oversaturated.

Return one finished image at the highest resolution you can.
```

## Checklist for every result (zoom in before you accept it)

- [ ] **Labels:** every brand name and word reads exactly like the original. Compare side by side at full size, especially Cyrillic (ШАРМЭЛЬ, КОРОВКА), Polish (Śliwka, Toruńskie) and the Noyan label.
- [ ] **Same products:** nothing added, removed or moved, apart from what the prompt asked.
- [ ] **No prices or people** anywhere.
- [ ] **Clean repairs:** where something was removed, the shelf, basket weave, wall or floor continues naturally, with no blur patch.
- [ ] **Photographic:** wood, wicker, marble and packaging look real, not plastic or CGI.
- [ ] **Still their store:** the owner would recognise it straight away as their shop.

If a result fails any box, don't use it. Keeping the original is better than using a fake-looking edit.
